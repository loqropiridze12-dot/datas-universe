Chapter I · The person behind the legend

A life shaped by questions.

In the world of this story, Data was remembered not because he possessed every answer, but because he refused to stop asking questions. His journey became a symbol of curiosity, learning, and the courage to imagine something better.

⌁

The curious beginning

According to the fictional archive, Data grew up in a small riverside town where old maps, broken instruments, and forgotten books fascinated him.

✧

A restless inventor

He filled notebooks with sketches and questions. His early experiments were imperfect, but each failure taught him to observe more carefully.

◎

A lasting idea

Data believed knowledge should be shared. In the legend, his greatest achievement was inspiring others to become explorers of their own world.

Chapter III · Tales from the legend

Stories from Data's world.

Three short fictional accounts explore the values associated with the character: curiosity, patience, and sharing knowledge.

Legend 01 · Curiosity

The Clock That Forgot Time

In a quiet workshop, Data found a clock that had stopped at midnight. Rather than repairing it immediately, he studied its gears, asking what each piece was designed to do. By sunset, he had learned more from the silent clock than from a hundred working ones.

Legend 02 · Courage

The Map Beyond the Mountains

A mapmaker told Data that the mountains marked the edge of the known world. Data did not claim the map was wrong; he simply asked how anyone could know without looking. His expedition returned with new sketches—and even more questions.

Legend 03 · Generosity

The Library of Borrowed Stars

Data imagined a library where every visitor could borrow an idea and return with a better one. The library became the heart of his legend: a place where learning was not guarded like treasure, but passed from person to person.

Chapter IV · The archive

What the legend teaches.

“A discovery is not the end of a question. It is the beginning of a better one.”

— A fictional quotation attributed to Data

Think critically

Ask where information comes from. Compare explanations and look for evidence before accepting a claim.

Learn through discovery

Observe, experiment safely, take notes, and treat mistakes as opportunities to improve your understanding.

Share what you learn

Explain ideas clearly and respectfully. Knowledge becomes more useful when other people can explore it too.
